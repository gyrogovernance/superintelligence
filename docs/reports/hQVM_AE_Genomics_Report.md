# hQVM AE Genomics Program

Every method in the RNA design literature weighs candidate sequences by one of three kinds of measure: uniform sampling [2], thermodynamic energy, or a prior learned from biological families [7]. This program occupies a fourth seat. Its models were trained on a formal grammar with no biological data, so agreement with biology could not be evidence. Deviation from the grammar is the signal.

This report records what the program measured and how each result is bounded. Program design and methodology live in the program specification; this document is the record of outcomes.

## Repository and reproducibility

This report describes genomics results from the [gyrogovernance/superintelligence](https://github.com/gyrogovernance/superintelligence) repository. The analysis code is under `src/tools/autoencoder/programs/genomics/`. The synthesis evaluations are in `synthesis/`, and the membrane-topology and fixed-peptide analyses are in `topology/`. The shared hQVM autoencoder package, including models, kernel adapters, and production checkpoints, is under `src/tools/autoencoder/`. Entry points, data ingest, and result files are documented in that tree.

## Scientific position

The hQVM AE Genomics program combines an exact coordinate system for coding DNA with autoencoders trained only on sequences generated by that system. The coordinate system represents nucleotides and codons as exact algebraic states. Adjacent codons form ordered transitions, and the kernel assigns each transition a local cost called shell. A coding sequence is a path through these states, and the kernel computes exact properties of that path. The autoencoders learn to reconstruct masked positions in grammar-generated paths and provide learned summaries of sequence order.

Small effects are the expected outcome here. The field's family-trained measures are fitted to the biological family itself, and they still leave most of the variance in measured function unexplained [7, 8]. Against that ceiling, an instrument with no route to the answer can be expected to find a small but coherent effect, and the Gamble library sets the same ceiling directly: its within-peptide expression spread is small, so the mean rank correlation of 0.0196 reported below is a proportionate ordering rather than a null.

Two properties of the coordinate system shape how the results should be read. Its cost function is a path quantity, the same class of object the field's kinetic design literature reasons with when it separates folding paths, barriers, and seed accessibility from endpoint energy. And the coordinate system was derived from the CGM axioms with no biological input, so the shell distances used here were fixed before any gene was read. The topology contrast is therefore an unsolicited biological consequence of that cost function rather than a fit to it. The derivation and its 301 verification checks are recorded in the genomics analysis [12].

This design separates the fixed coordinate system from learned sequence representations. The kernel is determined by the hQVM algebra, and biological data enter only during evaluation. That separation is what allows a grammar-derived representation to be tested against real genomes, protein topology, and synonymous expression.

## Results overview

The program reports three connected analyses. The synthesis suite evaluates the frozen models on genomic catalogs. The topology study tests whether adjacent codon-pair order is associated with membrane topology within individual *E. coli* genes and whether frozen model features improve held-out classification. The fixed-peptide study tests whether the same model readouts rank expression in a synonymous variant library. Each model claim below is measured as an increment over the kernel and composition controls.

The three analyses form a designed escalation, each holding more fixed than the last. The synthesis suite varies everything: whole catalogs, four organisms, composition uncontrolled until the comparison. The topology study holds the organism, gene, encoded protein, and host codon usage fixed and varies only the annotated domain. The fixed-peptide study holds the peptide itself fixed, so codon order is the sole free variable and the learned measure is on its home ground. That is why the fixed-peptide number is the smallest of the three and the right one to end on: it is the cleanest test rather than the weakest.

## Coordinate system and models

The hQVM kernel compiles a DNA window into a sequence of carrier states. It records exact path properties, including parity, complementarity, word signatures, and the shell cost of adjacent codon pairs. These quantities are computed from the coordinate system rather than learned from biological data.

Narrow and Super are frozen model components evaluated by the program. Narrow provides a compact representation of each topology window. Super performs masked prediction and supplies an aggregate window readout called climate. Randomly initialized models with the same architectures provide a comparison for the contribution of trained weights.

## Synthesis results

The synthesis suite evaluates K4, Narrow, spectral, and Super components on *E. coli*, yeast, SARS-CoV-2, and human chromosome 22 splice flanks. K4 preserves the required group symmetry with a maximum error of `3.32e-11`. Even-depth sliding commutators are the identity on every tested window. Odd-depth commutators are non-identity in `98.23%` of *E. coli* windows and `98.06%` of yeast windows.

The distributional comparison with the uniform carrier atlas is substantial. Total-variation distances for byte marginals are `0.1815` in *E. coli*, `0.1939` in yeast, and `0.2463` in SARS-CoV-2. The corresponding six-bit-state distances are `0.1308`, `0.1352`, and `0.1741`. These values show that the biological catalogs occupy a different part of the carrier state space from the uniform grammar atlas.

The order-memory test constructs sequence pairs with the same algebraic signature and byte multiset while changing only path order. Trained-minus-random cosine-distance margins are `+0.1931` on chromosome 22, `+0.2045` in *E. coli*, `+0.2250` in yeast, and `+0.2271` in SARS-CoV-2. The result shows that the trained Super model retains information about order beyond both the fixed carrier quantities and the untrained architecture.

Against GC-matched shuffles, Super climate reaches AUC `0.8408` in *E. coli* and `0.7113` in yeast. The trained-minus-random gaps are `+0.2124` and `+0.1052`. The kernel's own mean-shell statistic reaches pooled AUC `0.9190` in *E. coli* and `0.8336` in yeast on the same comparison, above the Super climate head. That the fixed coordinate statistic out-discriminates the learned head is a theory result: the grammar already carries the discrimination, and the learned head's claim is its certified increment over that analytic channel. The disclosure is our own kernel measured against our own head. Against protein-fixed synonymous resamples, the corresponding AUC values are `0.6619` and `0.6987`, with gaps of `+0.0931` and `+0.1296`. The Super context readouts clear the same comparisons. These tests show that the grammar-trained models respond to biological sequence order after composition controls are applied. This places the program in a familiar genre. The field's benchmarks publish instruments with open questions rather than solved problems and treat benchmarking as task comparison rather than truth, and a measure that ranks sequences by compatibility with a criterion is standard. The difference here is only the source of the criterion: the field's is learned from the family, and this program's is fixed by the grammar.

## Membrane topology in *E. coli*

The topology study covers 590 eligible *E. coli* K-12 integral membrane proteins with annotated transmembrane and cytoplasmic domains. For each gene, transmembrane windows are compared with cytoplasmic windows from the same coding sequence. The organism, gene, encoded protein, and host codon-usage table are shared. The annotated topological domain is the variable being tested.

The kernel statistic is the mean shell of adjacent codon pairs. The transmembrane-minus-cytoplasmic difference is `-0.2262` shell units. The contrast is negative in 492 of 590 genes, and the paired sign-flip permutation test gives `p = 0.0002`.

An amino-acid-preserving null redraws codons while keeping the encoded protein fixed. Its mean contrast is `-0.1825`, leaving an observed-minus-null residual of `-0.0436` with `p = 0.0002`. A usage-matched null samples synonymous codons using the host's observed codon frequencies. It reduces the residual to `-0.0234`, with 333 of 590 genes negative and `p = 0.0002`. The usage-matched residual is significant but falls below the pre-registered effect-size bar that the amino-acid-preserving residual clears. The gated claim rests on the amino-acid-preserving residual; the usage-matched comparison is reported for the host-usage question.

Boundary composition was examined in two ways. Linear adjustment for codon-boundary dinucleotide content leaves an adjusted contrast of `-0.0387` with `p = 0.0002`. Exact preservation of every boundary dinucleotide leaves a residual of `-0.0011` with `p = 0.2064` and allows fewer synonymous codons to vary. The two controls answer different questions: the linear adjustment tests a residual after a broad composition correction, while the exact match tests a much stricter constraint with less statistical power. The exact match is close to empty by construction: pinning every junction dinucleotide fixes the first and third bases of the interior codons, and the standard code places its synonymous freedom in exactly those positions, so the control forbids nearly all synonymous variation and cannot detect a synonymous-order effect. Its near-zero residual corroborates that the signal sits in the codon-identity channel rather than in junction composition.

Length matching pairs each transmembrane window with the closest cytoplasmic window from the same gene and keeps length differences within 10%. Across 1,854 pairs, the shell contrast is `-0.2105`, 70.3% of pairs are negative, and `p = 0.0005`. About 93% of the original contrast remains under this matching.

The data support an association between adjacent codon-pair order and annotated membrane topology within genes. The result does not by itself establish thermodynamic stability, folding kinetics, insertion mechanism, or a causal effect.

## Frozen model reads on the topology cohort

Each topology window also has frozen Narrow and Super readouts. Narrow heterogeneity has a mean transmembrane-minus-cytoplasmic difference of `-0.0214`. Super climate has a mean difference of `+0.0422`.

The held-out analysis uses 5,684 windows from 590 genes, including 4,059 transmembrane and 1,625 cytoplasmic windows. Genes are kept together in cross-validation folds so that windows from one gene do not appear on both sides of the train-test split. AUC is a rank-based classification measure.

The baseline contains kernel shell, GC content, window length, and the model read-truncation flag. Its mean held-out AUC is `0.758432`. Adding Narrow heterogeneity and the model walk length raises AUC to `0.767527`. The incremental AUC is `+0.009094`, with standard deviation `0.000320` across ten grouped 10-fold seeds. All ten seeds are positive, and the paired two-sided permutation test gives `p = 0.0019`. For the 5,594 windows that remain within the frozen read length, the increment is `+0.009521`. The 90 longer windows are reported separately.

Narrow supplies the main unique contribution over the controlled baseline. Adding Super to Narrow gives `+0.009958`. Super alone gives `+0.000708`. K4 supplies the exact symmetry certificate and the splice-alignment floor.

The held-out increment means that a representation learned from grammar-generated sequence adds local order information beyond the fixed kernel geometry, GC content, length, and read policy. It does not identify the biological mechanism responsible for that information.

## Fixed-peptide synonymous order

Gamble 2016 Table S1, measured in yeast, contains 35,811 nine-base variants across 5,148 encoded tripeptides. Within each tripeptide, the encoded protein and sequence length are fixed while codon order varies, and expression is measured for every variant in the `synGFPSEQ` expression column. This design isolates synonymous order while holding peptide composition fixed. Adjacent-codon effects on translation are documented in yeast [10, 11], so the question here is not whether such effects exist but whether a grammar-trained measure detects them.

The eligible analysis set contains 28,504 variants in 2,597 peptide groups with at least five synonymous spellings. Frozen Super climate, Super context, Narrow heterogeneity, and kernel mean shell are scored before expression enters the analysis. GC content and codon-count entropy are reported as additional composition controls.

The primary readout is Super climate. Its mean within-peptide Spearman correlation with expression is `+0.0196` across the 2,562 groups that carry a defined within-group rank; the remaining 35 eligible groups have no rank variance and drop out. A total of 51.4% of groups are positive. The direction was pre-registered, since the gate requires a positive mean rank, so the one-sided sign-flip test is used, and it gives `p = 0.0038`. Peptide-held-out evaluation gives the same mean rank, `+0.0196`, and all five folds are positive. Kernel mean shell has a correlation of `+0.0066` on the same comparison. GC content is negatively associated (`-0.0613`), so the Super climate rank is not a GC proxy. Super context norm, Narrow heterogeneity, and codon-count entropy remain near zero on this construction.

The result supports a small, reproducible association between Super climate and expression under fixed peptide identity. It does not show that Super climate fully predicts expression or that the observed rank difference is the only cause of the expression variation.

## Interpretation

The three analyses address different levels of sequence organization. The synthesis suite shows that grammar-trained models retain exact symmetries and reproducible order information when applied to real genomic catalogs. The topology study shows that adjacent codon-pair order is associated with annotated membrane topology within individual genes. The fixed-peptide study shows that a frozen Super readout ranks synonymous expression above chance under fixed peptide identity.

Taken together, the results are consistent with sequence order carrying information across genomic structure, protein topology, and synonymous expression. They do not establish that one statistic explains all three phenomena. Each analysis has its own controls, estimand, and limits.

The kernel and the models carry different claims. The topology contrast is a property of the coordinate system, and it survives protein, usage, boundary, and length controls. The order-memory margin, the held-out classification increment, and the synonymous-expression ranking are properties of the learned measure, each reported as an increment over kernel and composition controls. Each component earns a distinct claim: Narrow carries the held-out increment, Super carries the order-memory margin and the Gamble ranking, and K4 carries the symmetry certificate. The kernel is the grammar and cannot be surprised by anything; the learned measure is the only component in the stack that can rank or locate sequences relative to that physics. The learned object is the same in all three analyses: one set of production weights, frozen, regenerable from the null grammar by anyone with the repository, and used byte for byte without retraining.
