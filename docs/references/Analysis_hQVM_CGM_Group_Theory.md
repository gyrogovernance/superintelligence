The hQVM therefore encodes physical structure through ordered words, finite transformations, and the residual holonomy of closed words.

The group structure establishes the algebraic capacity of the hQVM. It provides compiled word actions, exact routing, finite Fourier analysis, symmetry-compressed operators, group-equivariant computation, shell thermodynamics, central curvature, quantum-information lifts, and controlled approximation of continuous rotation composition. These capabilities define concrete routes toward computational advantage. They arise from the group structure of the algorithm and from the correspondence between instruction grammar, carrier geometry, and harmonic decomposition.

The foundational distinction is therefore between state closure and path closure. State closure concerns the endpoint. Path closure concerns the full ordered word that produced the endpoint. ## 3. The hQVM as an Ordered Algebraic Grammar

### 3.1 The instruction structure

The hQVM instruction is one byte. Its eight bits divide into two boundary bits and six payload bits.

```text
8 = 2 + 6
```

where:

- `8` is the instruction width in bits.
- `2` is the number of family or phase bits.
- `6` is the number of payload or transport bits.

The two boundary bits select one of four family phases. The six payload bits select which of six oriented dipole modes change.

The stage assignment is palindromic:

```text
CS  UNA  ONA  BU  |  BU  ONA  UNA  CS
```

The outer positions carry the source-frame role. The six internal positions carry the payload. The central fold joins the forward and reverse readings of balance.

This layout gives the byte a grammar. A byte specifies:

- a reference-relative mutation,
- a six-mode spatial payload,
- a family phase,
- a transition in the active-record cycle,
- and one provenance atom in the ordered ledger.

The byte is a grammatical unit of transformation. Its meaning is fixed by the current carrier state and by its position within a word.

### 3.2 Transcription against the common source

Every incoming byte is transcribed against the archetype `0xAA`.

```text
intron = byte XOR 0xAA
```

where:

- `byte` is the incoming eight-bit instruction.
- `0xAA` is the common-source archetype.
- `intron` is the instruction expressed as a mutation relative to the archetype.
- `XOR` is bitwise exclusive-or.

The archetype supplies the common frame for all instructions. The zero intron corresponds to byte `0xAA`.

### 3.3 The bipartite carrier

The hQVM state consists of two 12-bit faces.

```text

```

where:

- `state24` is the packed 24-bit carrier state.
- `A12` is the active 12-bit face.
- `B12` is the passive record face.
- `<< 12` shifts `A12` into the upper 12 bits.
- `|` is bitwise union.

The two faces jointly preserve identity and individuality. The active face receives mutation. The record face preserves the prior operational content. Gyration exchanges their roles during the transition.

Each face contains six oriented dipole pairs. The pair structure gives twelve bits per face while retaining six binary modes.

```text
12 = 6 × 2
```

where:

- `12` is the number of bits in one carrier face.
- `6` is the number of independent dipole modes.
- `2` is the number of oriented sides in each dipole pair.

The six modes are algebraic coordinates inside the three-dimensional and six-degree-of-freedom architecture. They do not represent six spatial dimensions.

### 3.4 The ordered transition

At the carrier level, one byte executes four related acts:

1. The byte is measured against the archetype.
2. The active face receives the payload mutation.
3. The active and record faces undergo family-controlled exchange.
4. The mutated active content becomes the new record.

A compact transition form is:

```text
A_mut  = A XOR mask
A_next = B XOR invert_a
B_next = A_mut XOR invert_b
```

where:

- `A` is the active face before the transition.
- `B` is the record face before the transition.
- `mask` is the 12-bit expansion of the six payload bits.
- `A_mut` is the mutated active face.
- `invert_a` is the family-controlled inversion applied to the new active face.
- `invert_b` is the family-controlled inversion applied to the new record face.
- `A_next` is the active face after the transition.
- `B_next` is the record face after the transition.
- `XOR` is bitwise exclusive-or.

The mutation and exchange stages have a fixed order. Reversing them changes the map. The algorithm therefore carries temporal structure through composition.

The byte transition reduces the full map to six independent updates on two-bit mode states. For mode `i`, the transition is:


- `uᵢ` is the active binary coordinate of mode `i`.

- `L0` is the family boundary bit acting on the active output.
- `pᵢ` is payload bit `i`.
- `Hᵢ` is the second family contribution assigned to mode `i`.
- `XOR` is addition in GF(2).


where:

- `Ω` is the reachable hQVM state manifold.
